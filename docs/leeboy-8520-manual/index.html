01 · Quick start

The paving day in twelve moves

Ninety percent of mat defects trace back to two things: an unmanaged head of material and a screed that was never set up properly. This sequence prevents both. Print it, tape it in the cab.

  1. Walk-around and fluids

    Engine oil, hydraulic level, coolant, fuel. Check tracks or tires, conveyor chains, auger flights and kickback paddles for wear. Look for hydraulic leaks under the machine. Full checklist in Daily check.

  2. Start engine, turn screed heat on immediately

    Electric heat needs 15–20 minutes to bring the plates to working temperature. Start it before the first truck is even called in. Confirm the generator light and every heat zone light including the extensions.

  3. Set paving width and level the extensions

    Run the hydraulic extensions to the job width. Lay a straightedge across the bottom of the main plate and each extension. Set the extension so the rear edge is flush to a hair high (1/32–1/16 in) and the front edge matches the main screed's angle.

  4. Set crown

    Set the lead (front) crown 1/8 in higher than the tail (rear) crown for most mixes. This lets material flow to the centre and stops centreline tearing. Crown or valley the rear to the job profile only.

  5. Block up and null the screed

    Set starting blocks equal to the loose depth (compacted depth plus 20–25 %). Lower the screed onto the blocks, then run the thickness screws until the plate sits flat, and add a quarter to half turn positive angle of attack. Screed in float, not locked.

  6. Set end gates and the joint

    End gates down on grade with spring pressure. On a longitudinal joint, the end gate rides on top of the cold mat with 1–1.5 in overlap and the hot side set 1/4 in per inch of depth higher for roll-down.

  7. Confirm mix temperature

    Probe the load in the truck bed, not the crust. 275–325 °F is the working window for hot mix. Reject or set aside crusted lumps. Log the temp for the job record.

  8. Fill the hopper and charge the augers

    Run conveyors and augers until mix reaches the auger shaft centreline across the full width, including in front of the extensions. Do not move until the head is built.

  9. Pull off the blocks at paving speed

    Start at the speed you intend to pave at and hold it. Check depth with a gauge 10 ft out, then again at 40 ft. Adjust in small moves and wait five tow-arm lengths before the next.

  10. Manage the feed, not the screed

    Set the feed sensors so augers and conveyors run nearly continuously (85–90 % of the time) at a slow speed. Surging feed is the number one cause of ripples, shadows and thickness drift.

  11. Trucks: hitch, never bump

    Truck stops short, paver drives up and hitches, truck stays in neutral with brakes off. Fold wings only when the hopper still has material in it. Never run the hopper empty between trucks.

  12. Stop clean, restart clean

    When stopping: stop the conveyors with a full head, kill vibration, leave the screed down. Restart at paving speed with no thickness adjustment. Rake any mark immediately and get the roller on it.

What the screed actually does

The Legend is a free-floating screed pulled by tow arms. It rides on the mix like a water ski. Three things set its height: the angle of attack, the head of material pushing under it, and the paver speed. Change any one and the screed seeks a new level over the next 5 tow-arm lengths. Every screed defect in this manual is one of those three being unstable.

Head of material

The pile of mix in front of the screed. Correct is mix at the centre of the auger shaft, held constant across the full width. Too much and the screed is pushed up and buries the augers. Too little and the screed drops and the augers starve the outside. Every fluctuation prints on the mat.

Angle of attack

The tilt of the screed plate relative to the mat. More positive angle means the screed climbs and the mat thickens. Thickness screws and tow point actuators both change it. Small moves only: a quarter turn, then wait 30–50 ft before judging.

03 · Controls

Operator and screed stations

Names follow the LeeBoy 8520C safety and operation manual. Your panel layout may differ slightly by year. Learn what each control does to the mat before you touch it under load.

Operator station (deck)

  • Key and engine controls with throttle. Pave at full rated rpm for consistent hydraulic flow to the conveyors, augers and vibration.
  • Forward, neutral, reverse and speed dial. Hydrostatic drive. Set the paving speed once and leave it. Do not creep the dial during a pull.
  • Dual electronic steering. Small inputs. Steering corrections show up as edge wander behind the screed 20 ft later.
  • Conveyor switches, left and right, with manual or auto. Auto uses the flow gates and sensors to hold the head of material.
  • Auger switches, left and right, manual or auto, with speed control. Independent sides let you feed a wide extension harder.
  • Hopper wing switches. Fold only with material still in the hopper so crusted mix blends in rather than dumping as a cold lump.
  • Screed raise, lower and float. Paving is always in float. Lock only for transport and blocking.
  • Tow point switches with gauges, both sides. Electric actuators, 0–6 in of travel. Refer to both gauges to keep the sides matched.
  • Thicker and thinner switches, both sides, driving the electric tow screws. These are your fine depth control while paving.
  • Vibration switch. On while moving for pre-compaction, off while stopped or it will settle the screed and leave a mark.
  • Truck hitch. Engages the truck's rear tires so the paver pushes the truck. Prevents bumping and roll-back marks.

Screed station (Legend HD)

  • Thickness cranks (flight screws), left and right. Manual depth control on the screed itself. One quarter turn at a time.
  • Crown and valley mechanism. Front (lead) crown and rear (tail) crown adjustment at the centre of the screed. Lead crown slightly positive for flow; tail crown to the road profile.
  • Angle of attack adjuster (HD Pro). Sets the base pitch of the main plate independent of the tow point.
  • Extension height and slope adjusters. Match each extension's trailing edge to the main plate and set the slope for the crown or cross-fall.
  • Pre-strike-off. Sets how much material passes under the extension's leading edge. Lower it to reduce push on the extension.
  • Extension in and out switches. Hydraulic width changes on the fly. Bring both sides out equally when possible.
  • End gates. Spring-loaded to ride grade. Set on the surface, with the spring providing down pressure.
  • Screed heat switch and zone lights. Generator-driven electric elements in the main plate and each extension. All lights on means all elements alive.
  • Extension power plugs. Extensions heat only when plugged in. A cold extension is the most common cause of outer edge tearing.
Two people own the mat. The driver owns speed, steering and trucks. The screed operator owns feed, depth and the joint. Neither adjusts the other's controls without calling it out. Uncoordinated adjustments are how long waves get built into a lot.

05 · Screed setup

Legend HD setup sequence

Do this in order, on level ground, with the screed hot. Skipping the levelling step is the root of most extension lines and edge tearing. It takes 15 minutes when done with the heat-up.

  1. Heat first

    Screed heat on. Do not level or null a cold screed: the plates grow as they heat and the extension relationship changes. Wait until the plate is hot to a quick touch across its full length, usually 15–20 minutes.

  2. Set width and balance the extensions

    Run the extensions to the job width. If the width is not symmetrical, note it: unequal extensions put unequal push on the screed and it will steer and twist. Fit auger extensions and tunnel extensions when the auger ends are more than about 18 in from the end gate.

  3. Level each extension to the main plate

    Lay a 4 ft straightedge across the underside of the main plate and the extension. Adjust the extension height so the trailing (rear) edge is flush to 1/32–1/16 in higher than the main plate and the leading edge matches the main plate's angle. Check at the inner and outer end of each extension. Then set the extension slope to match the crown you will run.

  4. Set pre-strike-off

    Set the extension's pre-strike-off about 1/2 in above the plate for standard mixes. Lower it if the extension is being pushed up by material, raise it if the extension is starving.

  5. Set crown

    Lead crown 1/8 in above tail crown for most surface mixes (up to 3/16 in for coarse or stiff mixes). Set the tail crown to the road profile. Zero tail crown for parking lots unless the drainage design needs otherwise. Verify with a string line across the plate.

  6. Set the starting blocks

    Loose depth equals the compacted depth plus roll-down. Use 20–25 % or the 1/4 in per inch rule. For a 2 in compacted lift use 2.5 in blocks. Blocks under the main plate at both ends and under each extension. On a joint, the joint-side block sits on the cold mat.

  7. Null the screed

    Lower the screed onto the blocks in float. Turn each thickness crank until the plate sits flat on the blocks with no rock, then add a quarter to half turn of positive angle of attack. Confirm both tow point gauges read the same, roughly mid-travel, so you have adjustment both ways.

  8. Set end gates

    End gates on the surface with spring pressure holding them down. On the joint side, the end gate rides on top of the cold mat, not against its face. Confirm the gate is not lifting the screed.

  9. Build the head and pull off

    Charge the augers to the shaft centreline across the full width. Pull off at paving speed. Check depth at 10 ft with a depth gauge on both sides and in the centre. Small adjustment, then wait five tow-arm lengths before the next.

  10. Read the first 100 ft

    Look for an extension line, texture changes and edge condition. Fix them now with the extension height and slope adjusters, an eighth of a turn at a time. What you accept in the first 100 ft you accept in the whole lot.

Roll-down reference. 1 in compacted → 1.25 in loose · 1.5 in → 1.875 in · 2 in → 2.5 in · 3 in → 3.75 in · 4 in → 5 in. Tender or high-binder mixes roll down more; verify with a core or depth check on the first pull.
Minimum lift. Lift thickness must be at least three times the top rock size for the screed and roller to work: 3/8 in mix ≥ 1.25 in, 1/2 in mix ≥ 1.5–2 in, 3/4 in mix ≥ 2.25–3 in. Thinner than this and you will tear the mat no matter how the screed is set.
06 · Paving

Running the pull

The screed only prints a good mat when its three inputs are steady: head of material, speed and angle of attack. Every rule below protects one of those.

Feed

  • Set sensors so augers run 85–90 % of the time at a slow speed. Continuous slow beats on-off fast every time.
  • Mix at the auger shaft centreline, all the way to the end gates. Half the auger flight visible is the picture to aim for.
  • Balance left and right conveyors so both sides of the head are equal. Uneven head steers the screed.
  • Never let the augers run empty and never bury them.

Speed

  • Set speed from plant delivery, not from how fast you can go. Use the calculator.
  • Steady is everything. A speed change is a thickness change 30 ft later.
  • Do not stop for trucks. Slow down and stage trucks so the hopper is never empty. Stopping is a stop mark, restarting is a bump.
  • Rollers should not be forced to wait on the paver, nor the paver on the rollers.

Adjustments

  • One variable at a time, in small moves. A quarter turn on the crank, one tap on the tow point.
  • Wait 5 tow-arm lengths (30–50 ft) before judging or adjusting again.
  • Measure with a depth gauge on both sides and the centre before adjusting. Eyes lie on hot mix.
  • Do not adjust while stopped and do not adjust at restart.

Trucks

  • Truck stops 6–12 in short of the paver, in neutral, brakes off. The paver drives up and picks it up on the hitch.
  • Driver raises the bed in stages so the mix flows, not dumps. Keep the hopper one third to one half full.
  • Fold wings only with material in the hopper. Never fold on an empty hopper: the crust goes straight to the augers as a cold lump.
  • Probe every load. Below 250 °F, do not lay it as surface; below 275 °F watch for tearing on thin lifts.
  • Ask the plant to load trucks in three drops (front, back, middle) to limit coning and segregation.

Joints

  • Longitudinal: 1–1.5 in overlap onto the cold mat. Hot side 1/4 in per inch of depth higher for roll-down. End gate rides on top of the cold mat.
  • Do not broadcast the excess overlap onto the hot mat; lute it off or push it back to the joint only.
  • Roll the joint first from the hot side with a 6 in overlap onto the cold mat.
  • Transverse: start on a straight cut face, block the screed to the finished height of the cold mat plus roll-down, pull off at paving speed.
  • Tack every cold joint face and the whole lane before paving against it.

11 · Shutdown and maintenance

End of day and service intervals

Cold mix left on a screed is tomorrow's tearing. Cleaning is part of paving, not an extra.

Shutdown and cleaning

  1. Run the hopper, conveyors and augers empty on the last pull. Fold wings to clear the corners.
  2. Raise the screed, lock it, and while everything is still hot scrape the plates, end gates, auger flights and tunnel.
  3. Spray an approved release agent on all mix-contact surfaces. Never diesel: it softens the binder and shows up as fat spots on the next job.
  4. Turn off screed heat and let the generator cool, then shut down the engine with a short idle for the turbo.
  5. Note hours, any leaks, and any control that felt loose or slow. Report it the same day.

12 · Safety

Non-negotiables

Hot mix and hot steel. Mix at 300 °F and screed plates hotter. Gloves, long sleeves, boots. Never reach into the auger chamber or under the screed while the engine runs.
Pinch and crush. Hopper wings, extensions and the screed lift can crush a person. Nobody between truck and paver during hitch-up. Lock the screed before working under it.
Electric screed. The generator produces lethal voltage. Heat off and engine off before touching elements, plugs or junction boxes. Wet plugs are a shock hazard.
Trucks. Ground guide at every back-up. Truck in neutral on the hitch. Never stand behind a reversing truck to signal it.
Traffic. High-visibility clothing, a traffic plan, and spotters on any lot open to the public. A paver in reverse cannot see behind it.
Transport. Screed raised and locked, extensions in, hopper wings up and pinned. Tie-downs at the manufacturer's points only.
